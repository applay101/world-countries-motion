# How to make an episode (for Claude sessions working in this repo)

Channel: **Zoom World Travel** (@zoomworldtravel), English. Every video is about one country, or a ranking/comparison of countries (then `country` is the main one, e.g. #1, and the others appear as map markers). The video itself and everything published with it (script, captions, title, description, tags) are in English. Messages to the owner (Hossein) are in **Persian**.

## YouTube agent checklist (Hossein: apply in EVERY video)
docs/youtube-agent.md is Hossein's YouTube agent skill (also installed as the `youtube-agent` skill). Every episode run goes through its steps, on top of all rules below: /yt-viral (pick a proven format) → /yt-script → /yt-hook (write 5 openings, keep the best) → /yt-title (10 titles, pick the best) → /yt-thumbnail (style different from the last 3) → render → /yt-edit (frame review, fix before publishing) → /yt-seo + /yt-package (description, chapters, tags, Short) → publish. In the Persian message, mention the chosen hook and title.

## Pipeline
Push `episodes/<id>/script.json` to `main`, and GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/render.yml`) does the rest: TTS narration, Whisper timing, music and SFX, Remotion render. It publishes `https://github.com/applay101/world-countries-motion/releases/download/ep-<id>/<id>.mp4` and commits `episodes/<id>/status.json` (status, size, log tail). Rendering a 4-minute episode takes about 15 minutes.
Check progress without auth: `curl -s https://api.github.com/repos/applay101/world-countries-motion/actions/runs?per_page=3`.

## Tutorial episodes (education)
Practical how-to guides for people from anywhere in the world, e.g. how to apply for Spanish residency online, how to book a police appointment (cita previa) for fingerprints / TIE, how to get an NIE, digital nomad visa steps, and similar guides for other countries. Use ONLY official sources (government sites such as sede.administracionespublicas.gob.es, exteriores.gob.es, inclusion.gob.es, policia.es) and say "rules can change, always check the official site" in the outro. Use `steps` and `checklist` scenes with a `map` intro of the country. Never show a fake copy of a government website; the `url` bar only shows the real address as text. Episode id e.g. `spain-how-to-book-cita-previa`.

## Choosing a topic (trending episodes)
First check `ideas.md`: if it has an unchecked `- [ ]` idea, use the first one (it may be in Persian), and mark it `- [x]` in the same commit. Otherwise:
Pick a country that people are talking about **right now**: news of the last 7 days, viral social posts, a major event, travel trends, a record or a festival. Search the web for it and confirm the story is real and current, from at least two reputable sources.
- Choose a different country from the last 5 episodes (`ls episodes/`).
- The episode id is a lowercase slug: `<country>` or `<country>-<topic>` (e.g. `japan-cherry-blossom-2027`).
- The angle is: hook on the current story, then a country profile that gives viewers context (where it is, numbers, geography, history, culture, economy and travel). Keep it neutral and factual. No opinions on politics, no partisan framing, nothing graphic. Wars, disasters and tragedies are covered respectfully, with no sensationalism. Never invent quotes.
- Every number must come from a source you just read (official statistics office, UN, World Bank, IMF, UNESCO, reputable news). Put the source in the `note` field where the schema allows it.

## Variety rules (important: viewers must never feel they've seen this video before)
Before writing, look at the last 3 episodes (`ls -t episodes/`, read their script.json) and make this one different on all of these:
- **Opening** (`segments[0].scene.type`): `globe` (Earth spins to the country), `dive` (camera falls from space straight onto the map), `flag` (giant waving flag with light rays), or `hook` (kinetic headline on a neon grid; best with a question or a shocking fact). For `hook`, give `scene.lines` [[DISPLAY TEXT, cueWord]], 4 to 6 short lines that slam in one by one, with numbers as digits, e.g. [["42.7 MILLION","Forty-two"],["VISITORS","visitors"],["A NEW RECORD","new"],["JAPAN","Japan"]]; the last line is the country name. Never use the same opening as the previous episode, and use `globe` at most once in 4 episodes.
- **Look** (`theme`): {"map": "navy" | "night" | "sand" | "emerald" | "parchment" (history), "accent": a hex colour}. Pick one that suits the country (e.g. sand + orange for deserts, emerald + mint for green countries, night + cyan for futuristic topics) and differs from the last episode.
- **Format**: rotate between a classic country profile; a countdown ("5 things you didn't know about X": put `"rank": 5…1` on five segments, so a big #5…#1 badge appears); "then vs now"; a news explainer that opens on the story; and tutorials. Vary the number and order of chapters and the scene types used (not always stats, timeline, cities, cards, bars in the same order).
- **Hook (most important)**: the first 5 seconds decide if people stay. Segment 0 is at most 2 short sentences (about 12 to 20 words) and opens with a surprising number, a direct question or a sharp contrast, never a slow intro like "Welcome to" or "Today we look at". Example: "Forty-two million people flew here last year. More than ever before. This is Japan."
- **Thumbnail (equally important; no click without it)**: one idea, readable on a phone. `big` 1 word if possible, at most 2; `sub` at most 3 words and must fit on one line; `badge` one striking true number; `top` 1 to 2 words. The title, thumbnail and first 5 seconds tell the same story. Before publishing, open `<id>-thumb.jpg` and check that it is clean, high-contrast and readable at small size; fix and re-render if not.
- **Thumbnail style** (`thumb.style`): 10 layouts, never the same as any of the last 3 episodes:
  `classic` (text left, country right, arrow) · `split` (big flag left, map right, name across the bottom) · `bignum` (one giant number in the centre; best when the badge number IS the story) · `question` (giant "?" + a question in `thumb.question`, e.g. "Why is everyone going?") · `versus` (then vs now cards: `thumb.left` / `thumb.right` = {label, value}, e.g. {"label":"2023","value":"25.1M"}) · `flagfull` (the whole frame is the waving flag) · `rank` (giant "#1"/"#5" from `thumb.rank` or `top`; for rankings and countdowns) · `spotlight` (country circled in red on the left, text right) · `steps` (tutorials: white checklist card, `thumb.items` = 3 short items) · `headline` (centred map, name at the bottom with a highlighter sub, round flag).
  Match the style to the hook (countdown → rank, then-vs-now → versus, a shocking number → bignum, a mystery → question, tutorial → steps).
- **Energy rules (the video must never feel slow)**:
  - Segment 1 (right after the opening) is a `map`, `stats` or `cities` scene with at least 3 spoken markers or cities, so the camera flies immediately. Never a panel there.
  - At least 5 `map`/`cities` scenes, each with 3 to 7 markers or cities whose names are spoken. A map scene with 0 or 1 marker is not allowed.
  - Never two panel scenes in a row (timeline, cards, bignum, bars, steps, checklist): put a map between them. At most one `bignum` per episode.
  - 570 to 650 words in total (3:45 to 4:15), 35 to 60 words per segment, so nothing drags or rushes.
- **Music** and **voice**: vary them too (`pulse` for most; `am_michael` / `bm_george` / `af_heart` rotating).

## script.json schema (copy `episodes/spain/script.json` as the template)
- `id`, `country` {name, iso2 (flag-icons code), isoNum (ISO 3166 numeric, 3 digits, as in world-atlas), lon, lat (country centre), capital}
- `voice`: Kokoro voice (`af_heart` default; `am_michael`, `bm_george` also good). `speed` 1.0. `music`: `pulse` (energetic, default), `spanish_guitar` (rumba groove), `ambient`, `spanish_gentle`.
- `thumb`: {style, top, big, sub, badge, + style extras (question, left/right, rank, items)}: the YouTube thumbnail (see Thumbnail style). `top` is a short hook in a yellow tag ("WORLD'S #1", "EXPLAINED", "STEP BY STEP"), `big` is the country or subject in 1–2 words, `sub` a 3–5 word promise, `badge` one striking number ("42.7M visitors"). Keep it punchy and true. It is rendered to `<id>-thumb.jpg` on the release.
- The engine is high-energy by design: the camera flies to every marker or city at the moment it is spoken, so put 3 to 7 markers in map/cities scenes and say their names in the text.
- `segments`: 11 to 14 items, about 550 to 650 words in total (~4 min). Each one is `{id, text, scene}`.
  - **Write numbers and years as words in `text`** ("forty-nine point eight million", "nineteen seventy-eight") so the TTS reads them correctly. Short, punchy, spoken sentences.
  - The first segment's scene is the opening (`globe`, `dive`, `flag` or `hook`, see Variety rules); its text names the country (e.g. ends with "This is <Country>.") so the title slams in on that word. The last is `{"type":"outro"}` and asks viewers to subscribe and join the next country.
  - Scene types and fields:
    - `map`: `chapter`, `view` {lon, lat, span (visible degrees of longitude, about 2 to 2.5x the country width)}, optional `labels` [[name, lon, lat, "sea"?]], `markers` [[name, lon, lat, kind]] (kind: pin, dot, peak, star), `river` [[lon,lat]...] + `riverLabel`, `big` [["17","label"]] (right-hand number chips)
    - `stats`: like map plus `capital` [name, lon, lat] and `cards` [{label, value, suffix, fmt: int|1dp|2dp, note} or {label, text}]. Set the view lon about 0.15×span east of the country so it sits left of the panel.
    - `cities`: `view`, `cities` [[name, lon, lat]]
    - `timeline`: `events` [[year label, short text]] (3 to 5)
    - `cards`: `cards` [[icon, title, subtitle, cueWord?]] (icon: a library icon like "fluent-emoji-flat:steaming-bowl" (preferred, modern colour emoji) or a built-in one: flamenco, clock, bull, tomato, flower, train, torii, bowl, mountain, star)
  - **Digital icon library** (in node_modules, free licences): `fluent-emoji-flat:<name>` (≈3,000 Microsoft Fluent colour emoji, e.g. fire, rocket, airplane, money-bag, chart-increasing, star-struck, exploding-head, red-heart, trophy, 1st-place-medal, globe-showing-europe-africa, passport-control, house, palm-tree, snow-capped-mountain, camel, sushi, pizza, steaming-bowl, beach-with-umbrella, castle, classical-building, bullet-train, speech-balloon), `lucide:<name>` (line icons) and `circle-flags:<iso2>` (round flags). List names: `python3 -c "import json;print(list(json.load(open('node_modules/@iconify-json/fluent-emoji-flat/icons.json'))['icons']))"`. Use them for:
    - `scene.pops` [[cueWord, icon]] on any segment: the emoji bounces in when the word is spoken (1–3 per segment, on about half the segments; they make videos feel modern, don't overdo it).
    - `cards` icons, and `thumb.emoji` (a big sticker emoji on the thumbnail and the Short, e.g. "fluent-emoji-flat:exploding-head" for a shock, "fluent-emoji-flat:thinking-face" for a question).
    - `bignum`: `value`, `fmt`, `suffix`, `label`, `note`, `chips` [], `cue` (spoken word that starts the count)
    - `bars`: `kpis` [{label, value, prefix, suffix, fmt, cue}] and `bars` {title, items [[label, value]], cue}, `note`
    - stats `cards` also take `cue` (the spoken word that reveals the card)
    - `steps` (tutorials): `chapter`, optional `url` (official site, shown in a browser bar, e.g. "sede.administracionespublicas.gob.es"), `steps` [[title, detail, cueWord?]] (3 to 6)
    - `checklist` (tutorials): `chapter`, `title`, `items` [[label, cueWord?]] (documents, requirements)
    - `era`, `split`, `journey` (see History episodes; usable anywhere)
  - A `cue` is the first spoken word of a phrase in that segment's text (e.g. "forty-two", "census"). Elements appear when it's spoken; without one they are staggered.
  - Labels and markers appear when their first word is spoken, so use the exact spoken name.
- Check the map coordinates. Render previews before pushing if you can: `python pipeline/prepare.py` needs `build/timing.json`, so usually just push and check the stills from the finished video.

## History episodes ("The ENTIRE History of X in 10 Minutes")
A story format inspired by fast cartoon-history channels (one example got 760K views, ~12× its channel's normal). Use it for the whole world first (the world, all empires, world wars, the Silk Road, money, borders), then big civilisations with global interest (Rome, Egypt, the Ottomans, the Mongols, China, Japan, Mexico…).
- Length 9–11 min: 1,300–1,600 words, 18–26 segments of 40–70 words. Episode id `<country>-history`.
- Look: `"theme": {"map": "parchment", "accent": <a deep red/brown/blue>}` (vintage paper map, sepia grain), `"flagOnMap": false` (history is not about today's flag), `music` "ambient", voice `bm_george` or `am_michael`.
- Story arc: hook with the most surprising fact (0–20 s) → 4–6 acts in time order, each opened by an `era` card → a climax (the biggest turning point) → a short "today" act → recap of the whole journey in 3–4 sentences → outro question.
- Scenes: `era` {year, title, sub, icons [2–4 library emoji], cue} opens each act; `map` scenes with `areas` show territory: [{ids: [ISO numeric codes of modern countries], label: "Achaemenid Empire (approx.)", cue: <spoken word>, color, labelAt?: [lon, lat]}]. Always write "(approx.)" in area labels, because modern borders only approximate old territory. Use 3–6 markers (cities, battles, monuments) per map, `timeline` for quick sequences, and `pops` with emoji (crown, crossed-swords, scroll, castle, ship, money-bag).
- More history tools (all work in any episode, styled vintage on parchment):
  - `routes` on map scenes: [{points: [[lon,lat],…], label, cue, icon?: "fluent-emoji-flat:horse" | sailboat | camel | ship…, color?}]: a dashed road that draws itself with the icon travelling at its head (conquests, trade routes, journeys, the Silk Road).
  - Marker kind `"battle"` (crossed swords) or any library icon as the kind, e.g. ["Persepolis", 52.89, 29.93, "fluent-emoji-flat:classical-building"].
  - `split` scene: {left: {title, sub, icon, value?, cue}, right: {…}, vs?: "VS"}: two cards slam in (rivals, before/after, then vs now).
  - `journey` scene: {stops: [[year, label, icon, cueWord?], …]} (4–6 stops): the recap road near the end.
  - `street` scene (full-screen cartoon town): {era: "ancient" | "medieval" | "industrial" | "modern" | "future", time?: "day" | "sunset" | "night", title, sub?, people: [role or [role, skin 0–4]], cue}. Buildings match the era (columns and mud brick → timber houses → brick factories with smoke → glass towers → neon future), parallax pan, clouds/stars, cartoon people walking, waving and talking. Great for "a day in 500 BC Persepolis" vs "Tehran today", POV moments and then-vs-now.
  - `crowd` scene (on the map): {people: [[role, caption, skin?, cueWord?], …]} (3–5): characters pop up with captions ("Kings", "Merchants", "Poets").
  - Roles: king, queen, soldier, merchant, farmer, scholar, explorer, sailor, worker, tourist, business, student, chef, doctor, kid, elder. Skin tones 0 (light) to 4 (dark): mix them.
  - **Map timelapse** ("every year" style): a `map` scene with `frames`: [[yearLabel, [areas…], cueWord?], …] (3–8 frames): a big year counter slams in and the territory switches on each spoken year. Very popular format ("History of X: Every Year").
  - `era` cards are drawn on an unrolling parchment scroll with a wax seal; chapter titles, labels and area names use the Cinzel serif font.
- Facts: only well-established history from reputable sources (Britannica, UNESCO, academic or museum sites, national archives). Dates as words in text ("five hundred fifty BC"). Neutral wording for wars, religion and modern politics; nothing graphic; no judgement of present-day governments.
- Thumbnail: `rank` or `headline` or `flagfull` style with a strong emoji (crown, crossed-swords, exploding-head); title like "The ENTIRE History of the World in 10 Minutes".

## Other countries on the map (always do this)
Whenever the narration names another country on a map/stats/cities scene (a neighbour, a rival, a source of tourists…), that country lights up the moment its name is spoken: it fills with its own flag, gets a glowing outline that draws itself and a round-flag badge. This is automatic for every label or marker whose name is a country (e.g. ["China", 104, 35, "dot"]), and you can add more with `"countries": ["China", "South Korea"]` on the scene. Use the spoken name (aliases like "the United States", "UK", "UAE" work). Never just print another country's name without highlighting it.

## Data graphics (world-scale videos; work on any map scene)
- `arcs`: [{from: [lon,lat], to: [lon,lat], label?, cue, color?}]: glowing curved flight/trade/migration lines with a travelling light and pulsing ends.
- `bubbles`: [[name, lon, lat, value, iso2, cueWord?], …] + optional `bubbleFmt` ("{v}M"): round flags sized by the value, pushed apart so they never overlap ("countries resized by population").
- `columns`: [[name, lon, lat, value, label?], …]: 3D bars rising out of the map (tourists, GDP, height of buildings). Keep the view's lat a little north so the tallest bar fits.
- Scenes with arcs/bubbles/columns/frames keep the whole view (no camera fly-in), so leave `markers` empty there.
- `race` scene (bar chart race): {title, years: ["1950","1975","2000","2025"], items: [[name, iso2, [value per year]]], fmt: "int"|"1dp", suffix?, cue}: bars with round flags grow and swap places over the years; give the segment enough words (8+ s). Data only from official sources (UN, World Bank, IMF).
- Round flags for every country are always available: "circle-flags:<iso2>".

## YouTube growth rules (from current best practice; apply to every episode)
- **Packaging decides reach.** YouTube first tests a video on a small audience; strong CTR (thumbnail + title) and strong retention in the first 30–60 s get it pushed wider. Title, thumbnail and hook must promise the same thing, and the video must deliver it quickly.
- **Title**: under 60 characters, the main keyword (country/topic) in the first words, a number or a specific promise where it fits ("5 Reasons Japan Is the World's #1 Travel Country", "Why 90% of Canadians Live Near the US"). No clickbait that the video doesn't deliver.
- **Description**: the first two lines repeat the hook with the main keywords (they show in search), then "▶ Watch next:" with the link to the most related earlier episode, then chapters (from status.json), key facts, sources, and exactly 3 hashtags at the end (#Country #Travel #Geography style).
- **Tags**: 10–15, starting with the exact title keywords.
- **Outro**: ask one specific question viewers can answer in the comments ("Which reason surprised you most?"), then subscribe. If an earlier related episode exists, name it ("Next, watch our video on Spain") and link it in "Watch next".
- **Series**: formats repeat as series (countdowns, "Why…", "X vs Y", tutorials) so returning viewers recognise them; keep the brand (fonts, channel name, colours of tags) consistent while layouts vary.
- **Shorts = discovery funnel**: every render also makes `<id>-short.mp4` (hook + best segments, ≤ 57 s, vertical, "FULL VIDEO ON THE CHANNEL" button). Optional script field `"short": {"title": "...", "sub": "...", "segments": ["id", ...]}` (title/sub for the top band; ≤ 3 words sub). Publish the Short 3 hours after the main video (see below).
